The route so far

From a factory floor in Gujarat to a dining room in Birmingham

It started on a factory floor. At an engineering works in Gujarat that made spinning rings for textile mills, a slow machine or a late supplier showed in the day's output by evening. That job taught me to find the bottleneck first.

An MBA at Aston University brought me to Birmingham in 2022, along with a role at a Greggs franchise. Within months I was running the site without a store manager and covering two more each week. Most products arrived ready to bake, and the skill was baking them off to what guests bought.

Fresh Bake in Leicester was a new brand's first store. We wrote the routines from nothing, from the learning plan to the opening and closing checks. When the manager left, I ran the store alone.

Shake Shack trained me at Canary Wharf. Its service standards come from fine dining: a host at the door, food run to the table, every complaint handled at the table.

Then we opened Shake Shack in Birmingham, with queues outside for two weeks. Dubai is the next step, at a company growing homegrown brands across the Gulf.

Five cases

A big group, a central kitchen, a sales routine, a fair rota and a guest

Case 1 · Pressure

Fifty-two guests, one countdown

A group of 52 booked ahead at Shake Shack Birmingham. I asked for each person's order five hours before arrival. The orders came two hours before, so I briefed the team and planned the service backwards from the moment they would walk in.

Stations stocked at 30 minutes. Chicken battered and ready for the fryer at 15. Buns, shakes and fries started at 5. Beef went on only once they arrived, because it cools fast. Three-quarters of the team ran the group while the rest kept serving everyone else.

ResultEveryone was served by the time the group had settled, and no other guest waited longer than usual.

Used nextAny large booking: plan backwards from the arrival time.

Case 2 · Systems

Food from a central kitchen, finished on site

At Greggs most products came from central production and were baked off in store. At Fresh Bake, frozen and part-baked products and ready-made fillings arrived from outside. In both, the outlet's job was to finish food to the real pattern of sales.

The system suggested baking more than we sold, so I changed the rule. Instead of five of everything going cold, a new batch went in when two were left. Breakfast items ran until 11:00, with more baked at peak and less after 14:00.

The kit had to keep up. Ovens and fridges were checked at every opening and close, faults were logged and chased until fixed, planned maintenance stayed on schedule, and every repair quote was checked before it was approved.

ResultBestsellers were on display from opening, and far less food went cold on the shelf.

Used nextWherever food arrives ready-made, I set production to the sales pattern, not to the default.

Case 3 · Sales

Selling more on an ordinary shift

Promotion days fill a restaurant, but ordinary shifts pay the bills. I tracked average spend week on week, set add-on targets for each shift and checked them before the team went home. After every promotion day, the sales went next to an ordinary day's, so we knew what the offer had added.

Some promotions were built to bring guests back. At a university event I led, competition winners took home a keychain with a QR code worth a buy-one-get-one, once a day for a year. Students came in and redeemed it at the counter.

ResultThe team knew what each shift was aiming for, and promotions were judged on what they added.

Case 4 · People

A rota people can plan a life around

People stay where they can plan their week. The rota went out a week or more ahead. Days off were predictable, and back to back where possible. Earlies, lates and weekends were shared fairly, and the rota started from people's availability and the legal limits for younger team members.

Growth was visible too. At Shake Shack each step up the ladder was signed off on a real shift, run alone. Every successful sign-off was celebrated with a box of doughnuts for the whole team, a habit I carried from Canary Wharf to Birmingham.

ResultShifts ran on full teams, and people worked towards their next sign-off together.

Used nextThe first thing I would review in a new outlet is the rota against the plan.

Case 5 · Guest

An order on a sticky note

At Fresh Bake, a couple who could neither hear nor speak came in. Both were allergic to dairy, and the order was getting stuck. I gave them pen and paper, then showed them each ingredient one by one until we agreed it exactly.

After their second visit, I wrote their order on a note by the till and briefed the team. From then on, anyone could show them the note and serve them. On my last shift they came in to say goodbye and took a photo with me.

ResultTwo regulars with a serious allergy were served safely every time, by anyone on shift.

Six habits

What the team would notice in my first week

Before service

Forecast first

The day's sales forecast comes before anything else. Staff, prep and orders follow it.

On shift

Do it, then time it

Each new task I do myself and time, then hand over with a time limit and proper coaching.

Every 90 minutes

Walk the floor

Thirteen checkpoints from the entrance to the staff room. Problems found early stay small.

Open and close

Check the kit

Ovens, fridges and holding temperatures checked and signed, with any fault logged before service.

Daily

Read every comment

Guest feedback read each morning and shared at the briefing. Low scores get a call to put things right.

Weekly

Rota out early

Published a week ahead, built from availability, with weekends and closes shared fairly.
